# Dalmatian & Split Heritage Game Catalog

A reference catalog of traditional games local to Split and Dalmatia, with the information you need to decide which ones to ship in the MVP, which to digitize as online play, and which to organize as in-person meetups. Heritage status (UNESCO / Croatian Ministry of Culture) is flagged because those are the strongest hooks for both the hackathon narrative and a civic pitch to the City of Split.

## Tier 1 — Heritage anchors (UNESCO / nationally protected)

### Picigin
- **What it is:** A ball-and-skill game played standing in ankle-deep water. Players form a circle and keep a small peeled tennis ball aloft with acrobatic dives, kicks, and saves. No winner, no loser — pure play.
- **Where it was born:** Bačvice beach, Split — invented in 1908 by Split students returning from Prague who couldn't play water polo in the shallow water. Played continuously at Bačvice ever since (centenary celebrated in 2008).
- **Heritage status:** Inscribed on Croatia's national list of intangible cultural heritage by the Ministry of Culture. Often misreported as UNESCO — it's nationally protected, not on the UNESCO global list.
- **Why it matters for the platform:** This is *the* Split game. The Picigin World Championship is held annually at Bačvice and is judged on flair and acrobatics — the platform can be its digital home.
- **Digitizable online?** No — it's physical. But it's perfect for: a live tournament hub, video tutorials, "spot picigin happening now at Bačvice" geolocation, AR move overlays.

### Sinjska Alka
- **What it is:** An equestrian knightly tournament held annually in Sinj (≈30 km inland from Split) on the first Sunday in August. Riders ("alkari") gallop 160 m in under 12 seconds and lance an iron ring (the "alka") with concentric rings worth 1–3 points.
- **History:** Commemorates the 1715 defense of Sinj where ~800 locals repelled an Ottoman force of 40,000+. Codified by the 1833 statute. Only men born in Cetinska krajina (Cetina valley around Sinj) may compete.
- **Heritage status:** Inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010.
- **Why it matters for the platform:** The most prestigious heritage event in inland Split-Dalmatia. The platform can host the official live stream, run a points/predictions side-game, archive historical winners, and explain the ritual to tourists in three languages including čakavski.
- **Digitizable online?** No combat version, obviously — but a "ring-targeting" arcade mini-game would be a respectful and demo-friendly nod.

### Pljočkanje
- **What it is:** Throwing flat stones at a target, like a stone-age horseshoes or bowls. Dalmatian variant uses larger stones (up to 6 kg) over shorter distances (~6 m); flat throwing stones are called "ploke" and the target stone is the "lek."
- **Heritage status:** Inscribed in Croatia's Register of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2016. The Tocatì traditional-games festival (which features pljočkanje and links Italy, Croatia, Belgium, Cyprus, France) was registered by UNESCO in 2022 as a "good practice" for safeguarding intangible heritage.
- **Digitizable online?** Yes, easily — physics-based casual game. Also a great candidate for in-person meetup organizing (it scales from 2 players to a village tournament with minimal kit).

### Klapa singing (related cultural anchor, not a game)
- **What it is:** Multi-part a cappella male/female singing native to Dalmatia.
- **Heritage status:** UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage since 2012.
- **Why it matters here:** Not a game, but a deep audio identity for the platform — klapa-style audio greetings, victory jingles, and the "soundscape" of the app. Hands you instant Split authenticity.

### Splitska čakavština (Split čakavian speech)
- **What it is:** Local Split dialect, ikavian southern čakavian with a heavy štokavian overlay today. Carries the city's identity in vocabulary that has no exact equivalent in standard Croatian.
- **Heritage status:** Inscribed as Croatian intangible cultural heritage (designation Z-5902, "oral tradition, expressions and speech") in 2013, championed by the *Marko Uvodić Splićanin Association*. The first Čakavski susreti meetings were held May 2025 at the Dalmatina Library, focused on getting čakavski into Split primary schools.
- **Why it matters here:** This is the single best hook for the AI-tutor dimension. A čakavski-speaking AI grandfather teaching a tourist briškula is a heritage product that doesn't exist anywhere.

## Tier 2 — Daily-life Dalmatian games (the social fabric)

### Briškula (from Italian *briscola*)
- **What it is:** Trick-taking card game with the 40-card Triestine deck (suits: denari/coins, spade/swords, coppe/cups, bastoni/clubs).
- **Variants to support:**
  - *Obična briškula* — 2 or 4 players (pairs)
  - *Dupla* / *Dalmatinska briškula* — 2 players, each plays two cards per trick (the Dalmatian-specific variant; ship this prominently)
- **Digitizable online?** Yes — obvious MVP candidate, well-defined rules, fast rounds, ideal for daily leaderboards.

### Trešeta (from Italian *tressette*)
- **What it is:** Trick-taking partnership card game, also 40-card Triestine deck. Dalmatian variant is played to 41 points (vs. Italian 21). Communication during play is banned except for two signals: *tučem* (I'm cutting) and *strišo* (I'm done with this suit).
- **Why this game is amazing for the platform:** Those two allowed signals are pure heritage UX — the platform should render them as named gesture buttons, not generic "emoji reactions." That's the kind of detail judges remember.
- **Digitizable online?** Yes — second MVP card game.

### Karambol (also written Karambula / Karom)
- **What it is:** In Dalmatia, "karambol" can mean two different things — both played locally. (1) A finger-flick disc game on a square wooden board with corner pockets and a striker. (2) In some konoba contexts, a card-game variant. Total Croatia bundles it alongside briškula/trešeta/balote/picigin as a Dalmatian pastime.
- **Digitizable online?** The disc version is essentially carrom — well-trodden mobile genre. Ship the local skin (Triestine card faces on the central piece, Dalmatian color scheme).
- **Verify before launch:** The "Karambol as card game" claim deserves a quick local confirmation — ask a Split konoba regular which version they mean.

### Balote / Boćanje / Buće (Croatian bocce)
- **What it is:** Roll heavy metal or wooden balls toward a small target ball (the "bulin," sometimes "biloć"), knocking opponents away. Solo (3–4 balls each), pairs (3 each), or fours (2 each). Captain calls strategy and adjudicates measurements.
- **Origin:** Took root in Dalmatia in the area of the historical *Republic of Poljica* — east of Split above the Cetina canyon. So this is genuinely a Split-region game, not just generic Mediterranean bocce.
- **Cultural note:** Rules vary village-to-village by design; many players reject codified club rules to play "po starinski" (the old way). The platform should respect this by letting communities define their local rule set.
- **Digitizable online?** Possible (physics-based) but the social magic is in-person. Build the *organize a balote afternoon* meetup function first; add a simulator later.

### Mora / Šijavica / Mura (the shouting finger game)
- **What it is:** Two players simultaneously throw open a hand (0–5 fingers) while each shouts a guess of the total sum (2–10). Correct guess wins the point.
- **Heritage depth:** Roots in ancient Rome (*micatio* / *micare digitis*). In Italy: *morra*. In Catalonia: *morra*. In Arab world: *muharaja*. In Dalmatia: *mura* and *šijavica*. In Istria/Cres: *mora kantada* (sung mora). Traditionally a male konoba game, often played for drinks, with women historically excluded from even watching.
- **Why this is a hackathon hero:** It's voice-and-vision multimodal. A laptop with a webcam + mic can play šijavica with you. That's a demo judges will love.
- **Digitizable online?** Yes — *as a multimodal AI flagship*. This is the showstopper feature.

## Tier 3 — Other Dalmatian card games worth supporting

Quick list — all play on the 40-card Triestine deck except where noted, all are part of konoba/family life:
- **Bela / Belot** — partnership trick-taking, 32-card deck, very popular family game.
- **Šeš-beš** — Croatian name for backgammon in coastal/island use; "šeš" and "beš" are Turkish-origin terms for the die faces.
- **Cinco / Cingo** — shedding game.
- **Šaht / Šnaps / Žandar** — local variants common in konobe.

Don't ship them all at MVP — pick *one* (Bela is the strongest second-tier candidate) and reserve the rest as "coming soon" to demonstrate ambition.

## Tier 4 — Children's street games (the Povratak igri layer)

These are the games the Split-Dalmatia County Tourist Board and the Kinesiology Faculty in Split are *already actively reviving* through the **Povratak igri** ("Return to Play") program in 2026 (running in Split, Hvar, Makarska, Bol, Marina, Imotski). If your platform wants a guaranteed civic partnership conversation, this is the doorway.

- **Školice** — hopscotch (numbered chalk grid, hop on one leg).
- **Lastiš / gumi-gumi** — elastic rope skipping with progressive levels.
- **Trula kobit / Trula baba** — variant of the children's "rotten old woman" tag game.
- **Potezanje konopa** — tug of war.
- **Utrke u vrećama** — sack races.
- **Klikeri** — marbles.
- **Skrivača / žmurke** — hide and seek.
- **Lovice** — tag.
- **Pandurice i lopovi** — cops and robbers.

**Digitizable online?** Almost none of these — and that's the *point*. The platform's job here is to *get screens out of the way* and route kids into the squares. Event organizing, location finder, rule explainer videos in čakavski, school partnership mode.

## Klis — the wildcard worth investigating

Klis is a stick-and-base game (sometimes called "Croatian cricket"), historically played in Dalmatian villages. It barely shows up in tourist-facing sources but old-timers in inland Split-Dalmatia (around the *town* of Klis, just north of Split) may remember it. Worth a phone call to the Klis local heritage society before launch — including it would be a credibility signal that you're not just reading Total Croatia.

## What this catalog implies for product scope

**MVP online play:** Briškula (dupla variant), Trešeta, Bela, Karambol (disc version) — four card/board games covering most konoba evenings. Add Pljočkanje as a casual physics game in v2.

**MVP in-person organizing:** Balote afternoons, Picigin meetups at Bačvice, pljočkanje village tournaments, Alka spectator coordination in August, briškula nights at partner konobas.

**MVP voice/AI showstopper:** Mora / šijavica multimodal trainer — the demo that wins the hackathon.

**Heritage AI tutor layer:** All games taught by an AI agent that speaks English, standard Croatian, or splitska čakavština. Persona library: "Dida Frane" (čakavski grandfather from Veli Varoš), "Profesor Ćiro" (standard Croatian), "Tony" (English for tourists). The Marko Uvodić Splićanin Association is the natural partner for sourcing authentic čakavski.
